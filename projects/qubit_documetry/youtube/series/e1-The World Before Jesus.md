Yes. For **“The World Before Jesus”**, a 50-minute documentary can be divided into **5 chapters of ~10 minutes each**. The key is to make each part answer a different question and gradually lead the viewer toward the world into which Jesus was born.

## 🎬 The World Before Jesus — 50-Minute Documentary

### PART 1 — The Ancient World Before Rome

**0:00–10:00**

**Question:** *What kind of world existed before the Roman Empire dominated the Mediterranean?*

* Mesopotamia and the first civilizations
* Egypt and the ancient Near East
* Rise and fall of Assyria and Babylon
* Persian Empire
* Ancient Greece
* Alexander the Great
* Hellenistic kingdoms
* How Greek language and culture spread across the East

**Ending hook:**

> Alexander's empire disappeared, but his language and culture remained—and they would eventually shape the world in which Christianity emerged.

---

### PART 2 — The Story of Israel

**10:00–20:00**

**Question:** *Where did the religious world behind Christianity come from?*

* Early Israelites
* Kingdom of Israel
* Kingdom of Judah
* Jerusalem and the Temple
* David and Solomon
* Assyrian conquest
* Babylonian conquest
* Destruction of Jerusalem and the First Temple
* Babylonian Exile
* Return to Jerusalem
* Second Temple

**Ending hook:**

> But Israel would never again exist in the same political world. One empire after another would take control of the land.

---

### PART 3 — Judea Under Foreign Empires

**20:00–30:00**

**Question:** *How did centuries of foreign rule transform Jewish society?*

* Persian rule
* Alexander's conquest
* Hellenistic culture
* Seleucid Empire
* Religious tensions
* Maccabean Revolt
* Hasmonean dynasty
* Jewish independence
* Internal political divisions
* Roman intervention

**Major turning point:**

**63 BCE — Pompey enters Jerusalem.**

Rome has arrived.

**Ending hook:**

> Judea had lost its independence. But Rome's arrival created a new political world—and within that world, tensions were beginning to build.

---

### PART 4 — Judea Under Rome

**30:00–40:00**

**Question:** *What was Judea like when Rome ruled it?*

* Roman political control
* Herod the Great
* Reconstruction of the Jerusalem Temple
* Roman taxation
* Local rulers and Roman governors
* Jewish religious traditions
* Pharisees
* Sadducees
* Essenes
* Zealot movements
* Messianic expectations
* Tensions between Jewish society and Rome

**Important theme:**

This wasn't simply **“Jews vs Romans.”**

There were also major disagreements **within Jewish society itself** about religion, politics and how to respond to Roman power.

**Ending hook:**

> Judea was waiting for change. Some expected a king. Others expected divine intervention. And then, somewhere in Galilee, a new voice would emerge.

---

### PART 5 — The World Jesus Entered

**40:00–50:00**

**Question:** *What was the world like at the moment Jesus appeared?*

Bring everything together:

* Roman Empire at its height
* Augustus Caesar
* Pax Romana
* Roman roads
* Mediterranean trade
* Greek as a common language
* Latin and local languages
* Jewish religious life
* Jerusalem and the Temple
* Galilee and Judea
* Messianic expectations
* Social hierarchy
* Slavery
* Roman military power
* Daily life of ordinary people

Then gradually narrow the camera:

**Roman Empire → Judea → Galilee → Nazareth**

The final minutes should feel cinematic rather than encyclopedic.

### Final transition

> **Rome ruled the Mediterranean. Greek was spoken across distant lands. Jerusalem stood at the heart of Jewish religious life. And beneath the surface, a society was waiting for something to change.**
>
> **Into this world, in an obscure corner of Galilee, a child was born.**
>
> **His name would become known across the world.**
>
> **Jesus of Nazareth.**

**CUT TO BLACK.**

Then your next documentary begins:

### **Episode 2 — The Birth of Jesus**

---

### Recommended pacing

For each 10-minute part:

**0:00–1:00** — Cinematic hook
**1:00–7:30** — Main historical story
**7:30–9:00** — Major turning point
**9:00–10:00** — Dramatic conclusion + transition

This structure will make the 50 minutes feel like **one continuous story**, rather than five disconnected history lectures.

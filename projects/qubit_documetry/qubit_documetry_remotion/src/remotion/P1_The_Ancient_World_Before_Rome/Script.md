# P1 — The Ancient World Before Rome

**00:00–00:45**

Long before Rome ruled the Mediterranean...

Long before Christianity emerged...

Long before Jesus of Nazareth was born...

the ancient world had already been shaped by thousands of years of war, migration, trade, religion, and empire.

Civilizations had risen beside great rivers.

Kings had built enormous cities.

Armies had crossed deserts and mountains.

And empires had appeared...

only to disappear.

But their languages, their ideas, their religions, and their stories survived.

To understand the world that existed before Jesus...

we must go back.

Far back.

To the first great civilizations of the ancient world.

---

# MESOPOTAMIA

**00:45–02:20**

Thousands of years before Rome...

one of the world's earliest urban civilizations developed between two great rivers.

The Tigris...

and the Euphrates.

This region would later be known as Mesopotamia.

Here, cities such as Uruk and Ur grew into centers of trade, religion, and political power.

Writing emerged.

Administrators recorded grain.

Merchants recorded transactions.

Kings recorded their victories.

For the first time, human beings were leaving behind enormous written records of their world.

But civilization did not bring peace.

Mesopotamia became a land of competing kingdoms.

Sumerians.

Akkadians.

Babylonians.

Assyrians.

Each civilization built upon the achievements of those that came before.

And each sought control over the fertile land between the rivers.

---

# BABYLON

**02:20–03:35**

Among these powers, Babylon became one of the most famous.

Its kings ruled a powerful empire.

Its scholars studied mathematics and astronomy.

Its scribes preserved laws, literature, myths, and stories on clay tablets.

The ancient world was already intellectually sophisticated.

People watched the movements of the stars.

They measured time.

They developed mathematical systems.

They built monumental temples.

And they created stories about gods, kings, creation, death, and the destiny of humanity.

But Mesopotamia was only one center of the ancient world.

To the west...

another civilization had risen along another great river.

The Nile.

---

# EGYPT

**03:35–04:50**

For thousands of years, ancient Egypt flourished along the Nile.

The river gave life to the desert.

Its annual flooding brought fertile soil.

And from that fertile land emerged one of history's most enduring civilizations.

Pharaohs ruled as kings...

and were connected to the divine.

Egyptian builders raised enormous monuments.

Their priests developed complex religious traditions.

Their scribes recorded history, rituals, mathematics, medicine, and administration.

And their belief in the afterlife shaped almost every part of Egyptian society.

The pyramids were already ancient monuments when later civilizations looked upon them.

Egypt would survive the rise and fall of countless kingdoms.

But eventually...

a new power would emerge from the north.

A power that would transform the ancient Near East.

---

# ASSYRIA

**04:50–06:10**

By the first millennium BCE...

the ancient Near East had entered an age of imperial warfare.

One of the most formidable powers was Assyria.

Its armies became famous for organization, discipline, and brutality.

Assyrian kings conquered vast territories.

Cities that resisted could face destruction.

Populations were forcibly relocated.

And across the Near East...

Assyria projected its power through fear.

But Assyria was more than an army.

Its kings also built enormous cities and palaces.

At Nineveh, royal libraries preserved thousands of clay tablets.

Among them were some of the world's earliest surviving works of literature and scholarship.

The Assyrian Empire seemed unstoppable.

But no empire is permanent.

Its enemies united against it.

And eventually...

Nineveh fell.

The empire collapsed.

And a new Babylonian power emerged.

---

# THE PERSIAN EMPIRE

**06:10–07:20**

Then, in the sixth century BCE...

a new empire rose.

The Persian Empire.

Under Cyrus the Great, Persian power expanded rapidly.

Soon, an enormous territory stretched from Central Asia toward the Mediterranean.

Later rulers expanded it even further.

The Persian kings governed peoples of many languages, cultures, and religions.

They built roads.

Established administrative systems.

Organized imperial communication.

And connected distant regions into a single political world.

For the peoples living under Persian rule...

the empire could seem almost unimaginably large.

But Persia would eventually meet another expanding civilization.

From the west...

came the Greeks.

---

# GREECE AND ALEXANDER

**07:20–08:25**

The Greek world was not a single empire.

It was divided into independent city-states.

Athens.

Sparta.

Corinth.

The Greeks fought among themselves...

but they also developed ideas that would influence the Mediterranean for centuries.

Philosophy.

Political thought.

Science.

Theater.

Art.

And literature.

Then, in the fourth century BCE...

a young Macedonian king changed the ancient world.

Alexander.

In a remarkably short period of time, Alexander conquered the Persian Empire.

His armies crossed Asia Minor.

Entered Egypt.

Defeated Persian forces.

And pushed deep into Central Asia.

He died young.

His empire did not survive him.

But something else did.

Greek culture.

---

# THE HELLENISTIC WORLD

**08:25–09:20**

After Alexander's death...

his empire fractured into competing kingdoms.

But Greek language and culture spread across much of the eastern Mediterranean and Near East.

A new world emerged.

Greek ideas mixed with Egyptian traditions.

Persian customs met Greek philosophy.

Different peoples traded goods...

stories...

religious ideas...

and knowledge.

The eastern Mediterranean was becoming increasingly interconnected.

And somewhere within this changing world...

one small land would become especially important.

A narrow strip of territory between Egypt and Syria.

The land of Judea.

---

# THE WORLD IS READY

**09:20–10:00**

By the time the Roman Republic began expanding...

the ancient world was already connected by centuries of conquest and cultural exchange.

Empires had risen and fallen.

Languages had spread.

Religions had evolved.

Trade routes crossed continents.

And the peoples of the Mediterranean had inherited the memories of civilizations far older than Rome itself.

But the next great transformation was already beginning.

Rome was rising.

And eventually...

Rome would come to rule the land of Judea.

The very land where, centuries later...

a Jewish preacher named Jesus would begin his ministry.

But before that could happen...

we must understand the people of that land.

Their history.

Their religion.

Their kingdom.

And their struggle for survival.

This is the story of ancient Israel.
